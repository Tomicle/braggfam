Brew Me — the whole picture

Buy a friend a drink at a bar near them, from your phone. They get a text, walk in, and the bartender taps their screen to pour it. Warmer than Venmo, lighter than a gift — for the thank-you, the birthday, the "rough week, this one's on me." Brew Me facilitates the one payment and takes a cut; the bar gets a customer through the door who usually stays for two or three more.

How it works

Participating venues are Brew Buddies — not "bars," because the list includes any place that pours: cocktail bars, breweries, the burger joint with taps, and eventually coffee shops ("brew" covers both). Each venue sets a few price tiers (beer $8, cocktail $14) and gets a comp button on its POS — no hardware, no app, no POS change. Recipients are found by favorites plus a zipcode, never GPS: account holders pick favorite spots ("so people know where to buy you a drink") and save a zip; for anyone else, the sender types a zip.

Why tap-to-redeem works

The bar installs nothing and scans nothing. The bartender personally kills the voucher, so they know it wasn't a screenshot; every pour is timestamped server-side for the venue dashboard; the fraud ceiling per incident is exactly one drink. Pacing rule: one redemption per recipient per venue per ~3 hours.

What the account buys

Three tabs: brews (the wallet), send, and profile — where you set your zipcode and favorite spots. Push instead of SMS, and send one back in two taps. The poured screen prompts the thank-you drink — the highest-intent moment in the product.

The money

Stripe Connect: Brew Me is the platform, each venue gets an Express account (Stripe handles onboarding, KYC, bank details — we never hold card data or venue banking). One charge, split instantly. Venues are paid on purchase and drinks never expire — no funds held, no gift-card expiry law, no escheatment. A worked $10 example with a 15% cut:

LineAmount
Sender pays$10.00
Stripe card fee (~2.9% + $0.30)−$0.59
Brew Me cut (15%)−$1.50
Venue receives~$7.91

Tipping is sender-only: an optional tip at purchase rides in the same charge and passes through 100% to the venue — recipients tip the normal way, on the tab the 2–3-drink thesis says they'll open anyway.

If a venue leaves or closes

A venue that chooses to leave funds its unredeemed drinks (signup-agreement clause, collected via Stripe account debit) and the drinks transfer nearby at no cost to us. A venue that goes out of business can't be collected from — there, the transfer is Brew Me's unadvertised courtesy.

The venue pitch: a Brew Me drink is a customer walking in the door; the average visit is 2–3 drinks plus food, so the discounted first drink is customer acquisition priced below ads. Zero setup. The venue dashboard (web login, no install) shows every pour with timestamps, payout history, and the outstanding-drinks count; v2 adds trends and an average-spend proxy — the number that proves "the comped $8 beer turned into a $47 tab."

Warm craft: chunky lowercase wordmark (Fraunces at black weights — the font this page is set in; Nunito for body), a flat mug-and-pint mark with sage sprigs, soft rounded surfaces. Light mode is forest-on-cream, dark mode is cream-on-forest, amber identical in both. Redemption flips the whole screen to amber — the one full-color moment, recognizable across a dim bar. Voice is lowercase and warm: "first round's on me," "poured," "send one back" — the copy sounds like the friend, not the platform. No exclamation marks.

Why iOS-first still covers Android users on day one

The recipient web page means an Android user can receive, view, and redeem a drink with zero install from the first day — only sending requires the app. That makes the Android app a v2 growth feature, not a v1 gap, and the web drink page plus a simple landing page is the whole website v1 needs.

Open questions

  • Fee placement: absorbed cut (sender pays a flat $10 — current lean) vs. a visible service fee on top.
  • Alcohol law: voucher framing, 21+ attestation, and happy-hour restrictions need counsel per state before launch.
  • "Brew Buddies" naming: warm, but "buddy" can also read as the friend you're sending to — gut-check before App Store copy.
  • Redemption pacing: is ~3 hours per recipient per venue the right window?
  • Android timing and stack: native Kotlin vs. cross-platform, decided when v1 iOS proves the loop.
  • Brew Me's cut: 15% is the working number — validate against venue appetite in Sacramento pitches.
